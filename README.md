# Subtitle Processing, Cognitive Load, and Viewing Experience

This repository contains anonymised eye-tracking data and R analysis scripts supporting the study:

**“Exploring the Impact of Subtitle Types on Cognitive Load and Viewing Experience for Deaf Viewers in China”**

The study investigates how different subtitle types and language modalities influence cognitive processing and subjective viewing experience among deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) viewers.

---

## Repository Contents

### `/data/`
Anonymised datasets used in the analyses.

- `eye_tracking_data.csv`  
  Eye-tracking measures extracted from the subtitle area of interest (AOI), including:
  - Total fixation duration  
  - Average fixation duration  
  - Number of fixations  
  - Average pupil diameter  

- `questionnaire_data.csv`  
  Self-reported measures collected after viewing, including:
  - Perceived reading difficulty (7-point Likert scale)  
  - Enjoyment ratings (7-point Likert scale)  
  - Subtitle preference choices  

> All datasets are fully anonymised. No personally identifiable information is included.

---

### `/scripts/`
R scripts used for data cleaning, analysis, and visualisation.

Key analyses include:
- Descriptive statistics and visualisations  
- Non-parametric tests (Kruskal–Wallis, Dunn’s post-hoc tests)  
- Preference analyses (Cochran’s Q test, Bowker’s test)  
- Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) for eye-tracking measures  
- Repeated-measures correlation analyses  

Scripts are organised in the order of the analytical workflow.

---

### `/figures/`
Figures generated by the analysis scripts, including:
- Distribution plots of eye-tracking measures  
- Model-based smooth plots  
- Correlation forest plots  

---

## Experimental Design Overview

- **Participants:** 45 deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers  
- **Stimuli:** 18 short film clips (≈ 5 minutes each)  
- **Subtitle types:**
  - ST – Standard subtitles  
  - SDH – Subtitles for the Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing  
  - AC – Automatic captions  
- **Modalities:**
  - Intralingual (Chinese audio + Chinese subtitles)  
  - Interlingual (English audio + Chinese subtitles)  

Each participant viewed clips under both modality conditions.
